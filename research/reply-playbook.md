# After they reply: from reply to job offer

The ladder: **reply → conversation → referral or intro to the hiring manager → interview → offer.**

Every message you send moves one rung up. Every message ends with one specific ask, and offers two time slots when you're asking for a call.

---

## 1. They say "Sure, send it"
> Thank you! Here it is: omoniyiux.vercel.app/fairmoney (the 2-minute film is at the top). The short version: customers who repay on time get "try again in 0 days", and I redesigned that moment inside FairMoney's current UI. Would 15 minutes this week work for me to walk you through it? Thursday 11am or Friday 2pm WAT?

## 2. They say "This is great" or "Impressive"
This is your moment. Say what you want, plainly and confidently.
> Thank you, that means a lot coming from you. I'll be direct: I'd love to join FairMoney's design team, and this is the kind of work I'd do from day one. Who's the right person to talk to about that? If you're comfortable, I'd be grateful if you passed this to them or referred me for Experience Lead.

## 3. They give feedback or criticism
Thank them, make the change within 24 hours, and tell them. Nothing builds trust faster.
> That's a sharp point, thank you. I've updated slide [X] to [change]. Does that read better? Out of interest, how is the design team set up right now? I'd love to know where work like this would sit.

Then, in your next message, use the ask from #2.

## 4. They say "We're not hiring designers right now"
Don't accept the first "no". Offer two doors.
> Understood, thanks for being straight with me. Two thoughts. First, I've applied for Experience Lead, which is measured on cutting repeat complaints, exactly what this pitch targets. Would you put in a word? Second, if a full-time role isn't open, I'd happily ship the new-phone flow as a 2-week paid contract and let the results decide.

A contract is a foot in the door. Most contractors who deliver get offered full-time.

## 5. They say "Send your CV" or "We'll keep you in mind"
> Thank you! Here's my CV and portfolio: [CV link] · omoniyiux.vercel.app. Who will it go to, so I can follow up the right way? And roughly when do you expect the design team to grow?

If there's no news after 5 working days:
> Hi [Name], just checking whether my CV reached [person/team]. Happy to do a quick walkthrough whenever suits them.

## 6. They offer a referral
Make it effortless. Send a blurb they can forward word for word:
> Promise Olaifa is a Lagos product designer who designs and builds in code. On his own time he researched FairMoney's #1 2026 complaint (1,760 reviews, "can't borrow again"), redesigned it inside our current UI, and built a working prototype and film: omoniyiux.vercel.app/fairmoney. He's applied for Experience Lead and would be a strong fit for design roles as the team grows. CV attached.

Then thank them, and tell them how it goes.

## 7. They say "Let's talk" or book a call
Use the 20-minute call plan in the next section.

## 8. Silence
Send one nudge after 3 days, then move on. Don't take it personally. They're busy, not saying no.
> Quick nudge in case this got buried. The 2-minute film is at the top: omoniyiux.vercel.app/fairmoney. Even one line of feedback would help.

## The 20-minute call
**Before the call:** open the deck in Present mode with the prototype tab ready. Know your three numbers: 60 of 277, 22 repaid on time, 9 in 50 new-phone complaints. Write down two questions about them.

**Minutes 0–2: why FairMoney.**
"You bank the people I design for, and credit is a trust product."

**Minutes 2–7: the problem.**
- "The #1 complaint in 2026 isn't interest. It's 'why can't I borrow again'."
- "I hit the new-phone wall myself."

**Minutes 7–12: the fixes.** Show the prototype, not slides. Let them tap the "0 days" before-and-after.

**Minutes 12–15: your first month.** Run through the "first week" plan, plus the design system in a sprint.

**Minutes 15–20: their turn. This is the most important part.**
- "What's the biggest design problem on your plate this quarter?"
- "How is the design team set up, and where is it heading?"
- "Where would someone like me fit?"

**Close.** Never end without this:
> "What would the next step be for me to join FairMoney?"

**Within 2 hours:**
> Thanks for your time today, [Name]. I loved hearing about [one specific thing they said]. As discussed, [the next step, e.g. "I'll send my CV to X" or "I'll wait to hear from Y"]. I'll check back on [day].

## When you get an interview

**Expect three stages:**
1. A recruiter screen.
2. A portfolio review (30–45 minutes).
3. Possibly a design exercise or a panel.

**Portfolio review, two stories only:**
- **Quote Request:** shipped and live. You owned it end to end, in two weeks.
- **FairMoney:** this pitch. It shows judgment, research and craft on their own product.

**Be ready to defend every claim.** Before any interview, settle the 5 open questions on your CV:
- Was LeftLane part-time?
- Was Eonsfleet a contract?
- Did you build the 28 React Email templates yourself?
- Where does "bounce rate −30%" come from?
- The Moniepoint listing.

One shaky number can sink a strong interview.

**Questions they'll ask you:**
- "Tell me about a decision you got wrong." Have a real one.
- "How do you work with engineers and PMs?"
- "How would you measure this?" Use slide 22: grow, calm, protect.

**Salary:**
- Before any call, ask 2 or 3 designers at Nigerian fintechs what the range is for this level.
- If asked first: "I'm looking for a fair package for a senior product designer in fintech. What's the budgeted range for this role?"
- If they push, give a range whose bottom is a number you'd accept happily.
- Never accept on the call. Say: "Thank you, I'd like a day to review it."

## Your daily rhythm until you get an offer
- **Morning (30 minutes):** reply to every message within the same day. Speed signals seriousness.
- **Midday (30 minutes):** send 5 new connection notes. FairMoney first, then Moniepoint, then Watu.
- **Evening (15 minutes):** update the tracker. Name, stage, next step, date.

Treat every "yes, send it" as a lead and every call as an interview. You don't need 100 replies. You need one person inside who says, "You should talk to our Head of Design."
