Moniepoint: getting locked-out customers back to their money

I read 520 Google Play reviews of Moniepoint's personal banking app. One in five of this year's negative reviews came from people who couldn't get into the app at all: forced updates, failed face checks, and lockouts that last days. I redesigned the three moments where people get stuck.

RoleResearch and design, solo
Evidence520 Play Store reviews
PlatformAndroid · iOS
StatusConcept, not shipped
In short

The worst bank app moment:
your money is there, and you can't reach it.

  • The problem: 35 of the 158 one to three star reviews from 2026 in my sample are from people who couldn't get into the app. Several say they moved to OPay or PalmPay.
  • Why it matters to Moniepoint: a customer who can't get in can't make transfers, and every transfer carries a fee. Lockouts also flood customer support, the second most common complaint.
  • What I designed: updates that never block your money unless security demands it, a face check that coaches you instead of failing you, and a lockout screen that tells you when and how you get back in.
  • Honest status: self-initiated and untested. Below is what I'd measure, and how I'd test it first.
The evidence

What 158 unhappy customers
wrote about in 2026.

I loaded 520 reviews from Google Play and kept the 158 rated one to three stars and written in 2026. I tagged each one by what it complained about; a review can carry more than one tag. Tap a bar to see what people said, in my words.

Fees and missing money get more mentions, but they're mostly pricing and operations problems. Getting in is a design problem, and it's the one that stops everything else: you can't pay a fee, or chase a refund, from outside the app.

Constraints I designed around

A bank can't just
remove the friction.

  • Face checks are required. Nigerian banking rules require banks to confirm identity, so the face check stays. The fix is helping people pass it, and giving a safe fallback where the rules allow one.
  • Some updates really are urgent. A security fix may have to block transfers. The redesign keeps that, but makes it rare, explains it, and keeps everything else working.
  • Lockouts protect people. Pausing sign-in after wrong PINs stops fraud. The redesign keeps the pause and adds a countdown and a faster way out: reset the PIN.
How I'd measure it

If Moniepoint shipped this,
these numbers should move.

BlockedShare of app opens that hit a blocking update screen. Should fall to only security releases.
1st tryFace checks passed on the first attempt, and how many people need a fallback.
Back inTime from a lockout to a successful sign-in, and support chats about access.

What I'd test first: the face check coaching. I'd put a clickable prototype in front of 8 people with mid-range Android phones in normal indoor light and compare first-try success with the current flow.
