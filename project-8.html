Moniepoint: getting locked-out customers back to their money

About one in five of this year's negative reviews of Moniepoint's personal app come from people who can't get in at all: forced updates, failed face checks, lockouts that last days. I separated what design can fix from what belongs to engineering and risk, then designed and prototyped the parts design owns.

The evidence

What 158 unhappy customers
wrote about in 2026.

From 520 Google Play reviews, I kept the 158 rated one to three stars from 2026 and tagged what each one complained about. Tap a bar to read what people said, in my words.

These are the reviews Google shows as most relevant, tagged by keyword, so they show where to look rather than how big each problem is. Fees and missing money get more mentions, but they're mostly pricing and operations. Getting in is partly a design problem, and it blocks everything else: you can't pay a fee or chase a refund from outside the app.

Whose problem is it?

Design can fix part of this.
Here's where the line is.

ProblemLikely cause, not designThe part design owns
Forced updatesThe oldest app version engineering supports, and installs that fail on some phones.How updates are explained, what keeps working, and background updates by default.
Face check failingThe face-check software, phone cameras, and which fallbacks risk and the regulator allow.Coaching before the photo, and clear options instead of a dead end.
Locked outSecurity policy, plus bugs and support delays in the worst cases. No screen fixes a two-month lockout.An exact unlock time, what still works, and a reset that skips the wait.

Before building any of it, I'd ask Moniepoint's data three questions: how many face-check failures are bad photos rather than the software, how many update blocks were really security releases, and how many lockouts end in a support chat rather than a PIN reset.

Craft

Designed in Moniepoint's language,
tightened with the best of fintech.

A redesign pitched to a company should look like their next release. I started from Moniepoint's own visual language (the patterned balance card, white action tiles, the account-tier pill, General Sans and Inter) and tightened it with how Wise, Monzo and Cash App build theirs. Every blocking moment uses one modal shape, borrowed from talabat and Oportun: what happened, what still works, what to do. The countdown nods to Up, the face-check coaching to Lovi.
